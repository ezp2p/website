Chat with no servers in the middle.

ezp2p is a family of programs for talking to people directly. Your chats live on your own computer, your friends' live on theirs, and nothing sits between you that can read them.

Puddle

Your own chat, on your own computer.

Puddle is the app you run. It keeps your identity, friends, chats and files, and talks straight to your friends' Puddles. Your phone connects back to it.

About Puddle →

Pond

A place for a community to gather.

A Pond is a community server, with channels and many members. Someone runs one and hands out invite links, and people join from their Puddle.

About Pond →

Levee

A steady address for a Puddle. Design draft

A Levee is a relay for a Puddle that can't take connections from the internet. It gives the Puddle a fixed address and holds messages while it is offline, without being able to read them.

How they fit together

Two friends each run a Puddle. Once they've added each other, their Puddles hold a stream: a trusted, signed connection between the two. Messages, files and calls travel over it directly.

Ponds are where groups meet. A Puddle joins a Pond with an invite and keeps a copy of recent messages; the Pond keeps the rest. A Levee, once built, sits in front of a Puddle that can't open a port, so friends and Ponds can still reach it.

Everything they say to each other follows the Puddle Protocol, written down once so each program can be built and upgraded on its own. Nothing is public: a request that fails any check gets the same answer as a path that doesn't exist.
